# Chapter — Parliamentary System in India

## Topic: Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha and Functions of Parliament

Okay, class, let’s study this step-by-step. 👨‍🏫
This topic looks long, but it becomes easy if we understand **who represents whom, how they are elected, and how long they continue**.

---

# 1. Parliament of India

The **two Houses of Parliament** are:

1. **Lok Sabha**
2. **Rajya Sabha**

### Easy meaning:

**Parliament = the law-making body of the country.**

The two Houses work together to perform the functions of Parliament.

---

# 2. Lok Sabha

### What is Lok Sabha?

The **Lok Sabha is the lower and first House of Parliament**.

It is also called the **House of Representatives** because its members are **directly elected by the people**.

### Why is it called the “First House”?

Because the textbook describes Lok Sabha as the **first House** of Parliament.

### Election of Lok Sabha members

People directly elect Lok Sabha members from:

### **Territorial Constituencies**

**Constituency = an area from which people elect their representative.**

So:

**People → vote directly → elect their Lok Sabha representative**

---

# 3. Tenure of Lok Sabha

The normal tenure of Lok Sabha is:

### **5 years**

Elections are normally held after every five years.

These elections are called:

### **General Elections**

### But what if Lok Sabha is dissolved early?

Sometimes Lok Sabha may be dissolved **before completing five years**.

In such a situation, elections are held earlier.

These are called:

### **Mid-term Elections**

### 🧠 Remember:

**Lok Sabha → 5 years → General Election**

**Dissolved early → Mid-term Election**

---

# 4. Lok Sabha as Representative Body

Lok Sabha represents the **citizens of the country**.

The textbook states that, according to the Constitution, there can be a maximum of **552 members** in Lok Sabha.

To ensure representation of different sections of society, some seats are reserved for members belonging to:

* **Scheduled Castes (SC)**
* **Scheduled Tribes (ST)**

The textbook also states that if there are no representatives from the **Anglo-Indian community**, the President can appoint two members from that community.

---

# 5. Rajya Sabha

Now let's understand the second House.

### What is Rajya Sabha?

The **Rajya Sabha is the upper and second House of Parliament**.

Unlike Lok Sabha, its members are **indirectly elected**.

### Simple difference:

**Lok Sabha → Direct election by people**

**Rajya Sabha → Indirect election**

---

# 6. What does Rajya Sabha represent?

According to the textbook, Rajya Sabha gives representation to:

* **28 States**
* **9 Union Territories**

Therefore, its members work as representatives of the **constituent States**.

### Important idea:

Lok Sabha mainly represents **the people**.

Rajya Sabha represents **the States/Union Territories**.

---

# 7. Membership of Rajya Sabha

The textbook states that the total membership of Rajya Sabha is:

### **250 members**

Out of these:

* **238** → elected from constituent States and Union Territories
* **12** → appointed by the President

The 12 appointed members are usually experienced and distinguished personalities from fields such as:

* Literature
* Arts
* Science
* Sports
* Social work

### 🧠 Memory trick:

**Rajya Sabha = 250**

**238 elected + 12 nominated**

---

# 8. Is every State equally represented?

**No.**

The textbook says that all constituent States do **not** get equal representation.

Representation is **proportionate to the population** of each State.

### Easy example:

If one State has a larger population, it gets more representation than a State with a smaller population.

So remember:

**Rajya Sabha representation → based on population**

---

# 9. How are Rajya Sabha members elected?

The members are elected through the system of:

### **Proportional Representation**

**Proportional = according to proportion/share**

You don't need to get confused by the technical details right now. For this textbook section, remember the term:

> **Rajya Sabha → Proportional Representation**

---

# 10. Why is Rajya Sabha called a Permanent House?

This is an important question.

### Rajya Sabha is never completely dissolved.

Therefore, it is called the:

## **Permanent House**

But does that mean the same members remain forever?

**No!**

Members have a tenure of **6 years**.

Every **2 years**, **1/3 of the members retire** and an equal number of new members are elected.

Therefore, some members continue while others are replaced.

### Result:

Rajya Sabha can function **continuously**.

### 🧠 Super easy memory:

**Rajya Sabha → Permanent**

**Member's tenure → 6 years**

**Every 2 years → 1/3 members retire**

---

# 11. Age for Rajya Sabha Election

A person contesting election to Rajya Sabha must:

1. Be an **Indian citizen**
2. Have completed **30 years of age**

### 🧠 Remember:

**Rajya Sabha → 30 years**

---

# 12. Members of Parliament — MPs

Members of both Houses are called:

## **Members of Parliament (MPs)**

So:

**Lok Sabha member = MP**

**Rajya Sabha member = MP**

### What do MPs do for their constituencies?

MPs try to resolve the:

* Queries
* Complaints

of people from their respective constituencies by **raising questions in Parliament**.

The textbook also states that the government allocates funds to them for development work in their respective constituencies.

---

# 13. Functions of Parliament

Now we come to the second major part:

## **What does Parliament actually do?**

The textbook begins with:

### **Formulation of Laws**

**Formulation = making/preparing something**

So **formulation of laws = making laws**.

---

# 14. Formulation of Laws

Parliament has to formulate **new laws** to:

* Achieve the **welfare of the people**
* Fulfil the **objectives of the Constitution**

But Parliament does not only make new laws.

It also:

### 1. Repeals outdated laws

**Repeal = officially cancel/remove a law**

### 2. Makes necessary changes in existing laws

So Parliament can:

**Make new laws + Remove outdated laws + Change existing laws**

The textbook says that the Constitution describes the procedure for formulation of laws.

Parliament follows these procedures while carrying out this important responsibility.

# 1. Parliament's Control over the Council of Ministers

The Parliament does not simply make laws. It also exercises **control over the Council of Ministers**.

### What does this mean?

The **Council of Ministers has to function under the supervision of Parliament**.

It is the responsibility of Parliament to ensure that the Council of Ministers:

* Does not disregard Parliament
* Functions under parliamentary supervision

### Easy meaning:

**Parliament → supervises/control → Council of Ministers**

**Supervision = nigrani / dekh-rekh**

The textbook says there are **multiple ways** through which Parliament exercises this control. The details of those methods may be discussed further in the chapter.

### 🧠 Remember:

> **Parliament = Law-making + Control over Council of Ministers**

---

# 2. Amendments to the Constitution

### What is an amendment?

**Amendment = a change or modification**

So, **Constitutional Amendment** means:

> Making a change in the provisions of the Indian Constitution according to the procedure given in the Constitution.

The **Parliament decides whether an amendment should be made**.

A **Constitution Amendment Bill** is considered an important bill.

Parliament:

1. Discusses **why the amendment is required**
2. Considers the proposed change
3. Decides whether to **accept it or not**

---

# 3. Three Ways of Amending the Constitution

Your textbook gives **three methods**.

### Method 1 — Simple Majority

Some provisions of the Constitution can be amended by a:

### **Simple majority of Parliament**

**Simple majority = more than half of the members voting, in the ordinary sense used for the provision.**

---

### Method 2 — Special Majority

Some provisions require:

### **Special majority — 2/3rd**

So:

**Certain provisions → Special majority of Parliament**

---

### Method 3 — Special Majority + States

Some provisions require:

### **Special majority of Parliament + consent of more than half of the constituent States**

So the Parliament alone is not enough for these provisions.

### 🧠 Super memory trick:

**Constitution Amendment =**

**1️⃣ Simple majority**

**2️⃣ 2/3rd special majority**

**3️⃣ Special majority + more than half States**

---

# 4. Speaker of Lok Sabha

Now we come to a very important post.

### Who elects the Speaker?

At the **very first meeting after Lok Sabha elections**, the members of Lok Sabha elect:

* **Speaker**
* **Deputy Speaker**

They are elected **from amongst themselves**.

---

# 5. Functions of the Speaker

Lok Sabha functions under the **guidance and control of the Speaker**.

The textbook gives several responsibilities.

### A. Conducting the business of the House

After becoming Speaker, he/she has to conduct the business of Lok Sabha in an:

### **Unbiased manner**

**Unbiased = impartial / without favouring one side**

---

### B. Protecting members' rights and privileges

Lok Sabha members have certain rights and privileges as representatives of the people.

The Speaker takes care of these.

---

### C. Maintaining discipline

The Speaker has to maintain the:

* **Decorum**
* **Dignity**

of the House.

**Decorum = proper and respectful behaviour**

---

### D. Interpreting the rules

The Speaker interprets the rules related to the **daily functioning of the House** and works accordingly.

---

### 🧠 Speaker = 4 important duties

**Conduct → Rights → Discipline → Rules**

Remember:

> **Speaker keeps Lok Sabha organised and functioning according to its rules.**

---

# 6. Chairman of Rajya Sabha

Now, who controls the functioning of Rajya Sabha?

### **Chairman**

The Chairman exercises control over the functioning of Rajya Sabha.

### Who is the Chairman?

The:

## **Vice President of India**

is the **ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha**.

**Ex-officio = holding a position because of another position already held.**

---

# 7. Functions of Rajya Sabha Chairman

The Chairman:

* Maintains **discipline** in the House
* Facilitates **discussions**
* Gives members an opportunity to **speak**
* Exercises control over the functioning of Rajya Sabha

### 🧠 Remember:

**Chairman → Discipline + Discussion + Chance to Speak**

# Law-Making Process — Continued

## Second Reading and Third Reading

Excellent. Now we are at the **most detailed part of the law-making process**. Let's make it very simple.

### First, remember the full flow:

**Bill introduced**
⬇️
**First Reading**
⬇️
**Second Reading**
⬇️
**Third Reading**
⬇️
**Other House**
⬇️
**President's Assent**
⬇️

### **Law / Act**

---

# 1. Second Reading

The **Second Reading has two stages**.

## Stage 1 — General Discussion

First, the **objectives of the proposed Bill** are discussed.

**Objective = aim / purpose**

Members of the House express their opinions.

### Supporters of the Bill

Members who support the Bill give **favourable opinions**.

### Opponents of the Bill

Members who oppose it discuss its:

* Defects
* Faults
* Problems

### Easy example:

Suppose a Bill is introduced about improving education.

**Supporters:**
“This Bill will improve education.”

**Opponents:**
“There are some problems or weaknesses in this Bill.”

So, the House discusses the Bill from different viewpoints.

---

# 2. Bill Goes to a Committee

After discussion in the House, **as required**, the Bill is sent to a **committee of the House**.

The committee carefully examines the Bill.

It prepares a:

### **Committee Report**

The report contains:

* Instructions
* Recommendations

These are sent back to the House.

### Why?

According to the textbook, the purpose is to help make the Bill **flawless**.

**Flawless = without defects or mistakes**

### 🧠 Remember:

**Discussion → Committee → Report → Recommendations**

---

# 3. Stage 2 of Second Reading

Now the **second phase of the Second Reading** begins.

Here, the Bill is discussed:

### **Clause by Clause**

**Clause = a particular section/provision of a Bill**

So members examine each part carefully.

Members can also:

### **Suggest changes**

After this detailed discussion:

### **Voting takes place**

in the House.

### 🧠 Second Reading formula:

> **Discuss → Committee → Report → Clause-by-clause discussion → Changes → Voting**

---

# 4. Third Reading

After the Second Reading is completed, we come to:

## **Third Reading**

During the Third Reading, the Bill is **briefly discussed again**.

Then:

### **Voting takes place**

for approving the Bill.

If the Bill gets the required majority, it is considered:

## **Passed by the House**

---

# 5. The Bill Goes to the Other House

Remember, Parliament has two Houses:

**Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha**

After being passed by one House, the Bill undergoes the **same procedure in the other House**.

So:

**First House passes Bill**
⬇️
**Other House considers it**
⬇️
**Same procedure**

---

# 6. What if Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha Disagree?

Sometimes there can be a **difference of opinion** between Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha over a particular Bill.

Then the matter is decided through:

## **Joint Meeting of Both Houses**

### Easy meaning:

**Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha → Joint Meeting → Difference is decided**

---

# 7. President's Assent

After the Bill gets approval from **both Houses**, it is sent to the:

### **President**

for assent.

**Assent = official approval**

The President gives the final assent/signature.

After the President's final assent and signature:

### **Bill → Law**

So the proposed Bill becomes an **Act/Law**.

---

# 🎯 Exam-Ready Questions

### Q1. What are the two stages of the Second Reading?

**Answer:**
The first stage consists of discussion on the objectives of the proposed Bill and opinions of the members. After this, as required, the Bill is sent to a House committee. The committee sends its report with instructions and recommendations. In the second stage, the Bill is discussed clause by clause, members can suggest changes and voting is taken.

---

### Q2. What happens during the Third Reading?

**Answer:**
During the Third Reading, the Bill is briefly discussed again. Voting is then taken for its approval. If it receives the required majority, the Bill is considered passed by the House.

---

### Q3. What happens after a Bill is passed by one House?

**Answer:**
The Bill undergoes the same procedure in the other House. After approval by both Houses, it is sent to the President for assent.

---

### Q4. What happens if Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha have a difference of opinion over a Bill?

**Answer:**
If there is a difference of opinion between Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha over a Bill, the matter is decided in a **joint meeting of both Houses**.

---

### Q5. When does a Bill become a law?

**Answer:**
After the Bill is approved by both Houses and receives the **final assent and signature of the President**, it is converted into a **law**.

---
